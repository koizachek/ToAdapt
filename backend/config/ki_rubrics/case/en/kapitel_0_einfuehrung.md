# 1 Introduction and Notes

## 1.1 Notes on the case study

This case study accompanies you through the whole semester. It consists of five chapters, each to be read before the corresponding touchpoint. You read chapter A before Touchpoint 1, chapter B is added before Touchpoint 2, and so on. Each new chapter builds on the previous one and extends the information base you work with.

The case deliberately contains more information than you need for any single touchpoint. Not all details are equally relevant. The challenge is to identify the decisive aspects and to recognize the less important ones.

| Chapter | Read before | Focus |
| A | Touchpoint 1 | Discipline: Foundations (framework). Who is ON? Market environment, challenges, stakeholders |
| B | Touchpoint 2 | Discipline: Strategy. Strategic crossroads: where should ON grow? |
| C | Touchpoint 3 | Discipline: Market access & marketing. Pricing decision, channel decision, positioning and their coherence |
| D | Touchpoint 4 | Discipline: SCM / operations. Supply chain, make-or-buy, cost structure, sustainability |
| E | Touchpoint 5 | Discipline: Integration. Business model, design dimensions, reflection |

## 1.2 Note on working with this case

For all touchpoint assignments: only the facts, data and presentations in this document are authoritative. Information about ON from the internet, from annual reports or from other sources may supplement your argumentation but does not replace the case information. Where internet sources and case data contradict each other, the case prevails. The same principle applies in the exam: there, only the information in the exam case is authoritative, not that of ON. This also protects you from misinformation and ensures that all students work from the same basis. On the origin of the information: the company history, the financial and regional figures, the competitor figures and the regulatory details are based on public sources (SEC filings, On's media releases and Impact Report, competitors' annual reports, EU legislation); they are referenced in the text in APA style (author, year), the reference list is at the end of the document; footnotes contain supplementary notes and labels. Internal figures, surveys, price tests, contribution margins, budgets and all quotations from employees, retail partners and managers are constructed for teaching purposes and labeled as “case assumption”. Scenes, phase labels, working titles, internal debates and management assessments are case construction and are not labeled individually. All quotations marked as verbatim come from the sources cited. Voices and letters labeled as fictitious are teaching material without real authors.
