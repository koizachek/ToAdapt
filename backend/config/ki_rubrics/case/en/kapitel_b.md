# 3 Chapter B

Strategic crossroads: broaden or deepen?

## 3.1 The strategic question

At the end of 2025, ON has reached a point at which growth no longer happens automatically. The phase in which every new market and every new product generated revenue almost by itself is coming to an end. The company must decide deliberately where to direct its limited resources. Three strategic options are on the table, each with its own logic, its own risk profile and its own consequences for the organisation.
The first option is geographic expansion, internally called "Global Push": systematically building up China and South-East Asia. This is where the greatest volume growth lies, but also the highest risk. ON has no established brand awareness in Asia and no retail access via specialist retail, and it must compete against local players who know the market and consumer psychology better. The estimated investment is 200 to 300 million CHF over three years: money that would not be available for other priorities.
The second option is category expansion under the working title "Beyond Running": building up apparel, outdoor and lifestyle accessories as a second pillar. ON has already started with apparel and generates 18 per cent of its revenue there, but the collection is still narrow and brand perception in the textile sector is not established. Realising the full potential requires a dedicated supply chain for textiles, an expanded design team and a brand positioning that goes beyond shoes. The estimated investment is 150 to 200 million CHF.
The third option is deepening under the working title "Premium Fortress": penetrating existing markets more deeply, expanding DTC, sharpening the premium positioning, accelerating innovation and deliberately keeping distribution selective. Less growth in volume, but higher margins and stronger brand control. The estimated investment is 80 to 120 million CHF, significantly less than the other options, but with the risk that investors' growth expectations will not be met.
"I hate to say it, but China is a billion-franc money pit. We have invested 40 million and we are not profitable. The brand is not known there, the competition is brutal, and we do not understand the consumers. Every franc we put into China is missing from the DTC expansion in North America, where we demonstrably make money. I strongly recommend reducing the Asia investments to a minimum and deploying the resources where the return is visible."– Member of the Board of Directors, former portfolio manager"I hate to say it, but China is a billion-franc money pit. This position directly contradicts the stance of the Head of Growth, who argues that in ten years ON will need the Chinese market in order to remain relevant. The question of who is right cannot be answered today, but the decision on how much ON invests in China has to be made now.
None of the three options completely rules out the others, but resources are limited. ON cannot do everything at the same time. Every prioritisation creates winners and losers within the organisation, and each has consequences for the relationship with stakeholders.

## 3.2 The competitive landscape

ON operates in a market that is dominated by a few large players, but in which the balance of power is shifting. Nike, by far the largest company with 51 billion USD in revenue, is in a growth crisis for the first time in decades. A failed DTC strategy that reduced the wholesale channel too quickly, together with several changes of CEO, has unsettled the company. At the same time, up-and-coming brands such as Hoka and ON have gained market share.
The question is whether this shift is permanent or a temporary window that will close as soon as Nike recovers. Historically, Nike has always overcome crises: through innovation offensives, marketing power and sheer size. If Nike corrects its DTC strategy and at the same time invests in premium running, this could considerably narrow the space that ON occupies.

## 3.3 ON's resource base: What is hard to copy?

For every strategic decision, the central question is what ON's competitive advantage is based on and how durable it is. The CloudTec technology is protected by patents, but the patents expire between 2027 and 2029. Hoka has already developed similar cushioning systems. In the medium term, the technology alone is not a sustainable advantage; it must be complemented by continuous innovation.
The brand and the design DNA are harder to copy. ON's minimalist aesthetic and its Swiss origin create a brand perception that cannot simply be replicated. New Balance is attempting something similar with its "Made in USA" line, but the positioning is a different one. However, brands can be diluted, and that is precisely the threat if ON distributes too broadly.
The DTC platform and the customer data are growing and hard to replicate because they are built on proprietary data. The higher the DTC share, the more valuable this wealth of data becomes, provided that ON uses it systematically for personalisation, product development and customer loyalty. Finally, the innovation culture and speed are perhaps ON's strongest, but also its most vulnerable, advantage. ON brings new products to market faster than the large players: 18 months from idea to shelf, compared with 24 to 36 months at Nike. This agility is a direct function of the size of the organisation. It diminishes with every new employee, every additional hierarchical level and every further coordination process.
